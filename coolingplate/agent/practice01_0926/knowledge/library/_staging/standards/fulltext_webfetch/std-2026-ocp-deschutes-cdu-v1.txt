##### 2. Compliance with OCP Tenets

2.1 Openness

Google is contributing the fifth-generation cooling distribution unit “Project Deschutes” 
specification to help accelerate adoption of liquid cooling industry wide. This is the first 
CDU specification submission to OCP. The specification provides technical design 
details, manufacturing quality, maintenance and service procedures and identifies the 
supply ecosystem for system components. 
This version of the specification includes reliability and uptime, deployment procedures 
and operational excellence that are informed by the 7+ years of liquid cooling at scale 
experience at Google. 
Finally, one of the objectives of opening the specification to the industry is to collect 
end-to-end feedback to align to a CDU design that can be used widely in first and third 
party data centers. 

2.2 Efficiency

The Project Deschutes CDU is targeted to support ~2 MW heat loads which would be among the highest CDU thermal capacities available in the industry. Similarly, the hydraulic capacity targets of 500 GPM at 80-90 psi are among the highest in the industry. The performance targets are intended to enable more efficient cooling of the next generation AI/ML workloads.

2.3 Impact

The specification will enable any CDU supplier in the industry to develop, manufacture and improve upon the “Project Deschutes” design which is informed by more than 7

years of scale liquid cooling experience at Google. Furthermore,1st party hyperscalers and 3rd party data center owners alike all over the world would be able purchase a CDU based on the “Project Deschutes” specification.

2.4 Scale

The “Project Deschutes” CDU is assembled from components that are sourced from multiple vendors that are widely known in the industry. Custom components in the CDU are intended to be manufactureable by well-known processes. Beyond supply chain considerations, installation and maintenance procedures are shared to enable fast deployments of reliable equipment. The processes and best practices shared by Google are based on the 7+ years of scale liquid cooling experience.

2.5 Sustainability

Liquid cooling generally requires less electrical power and physical data center space to cool IT hardware compared to air cooling. As such, the “Project Deschutes” performance targets would enable cooling high IT heat loads in the industry with less electricity consumption and/or less data center space. Furthermore, since the specification is available to CDU manufacturers globally, data center owners would be able to source CDUs manufactured relatively near to their location.

7.2.3 Internet Protocol Dual-Stack IP Support The PLC concurrently supports both IPv4 and IPv6 network stacks. This dual-stack capability ensures seamless communication and interoperability with both legacy IPv4-only devices and modern IPv6 infrastructure, facilitating a gradual and non-disruptive transition to IPv6.

The controller shall read/write Modbus/TCP over IPv6 and IPv4.

IPv4

● Maintains full compatibility with existing IPv4 networks and devices. 
● Supports standard IPv4 addressing and subnetting. 
● Can be configured with a static IPv4 address or obtain one dynamically via a standard DHCP server. 

IPv6 The device incorporates a comprehensive set of IPv6 features

● DHCPv6 (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol for IPv6): The PLC can automatically obtain an IPv6 address, DNS server information, and other network configuration parameters from a DHCPv6 server. This simplifies network management and reduces the need for manual static address assignment. 
● SLAAC (Stateless Address Autoconfiguration): The device can independently generate its own globally unique IPv6 address based on network information provided by a local router. This allows for plug-and-play functionality on IPv6 networks without requiring a DHCPv6 server. 
● Neighbor Discovery Protocol (NDP): Replaces the broadcast-based ARP (Address Resolution Protocol) protocol used in IPv4. NDP is more efficient for discovering other devices on the local network, finding routers, and maintaining reachability information, resulting in reduced network overhead. 
● Expanded Address Space: With 128-bit addresses, IPv6 provides a virtually limitless number of unique IP addresses, eliminating the address exhaustion issues of IPv4 and allowing every device to have a unique global IP.

7.4 Power specification summary

● VFD is rated at 50HP (37.3kW) with a power factor of between 0.98 to 1.00 
○ Apparent power requirement can be rounded up to 38kW 
● Nominal voltage range 380Vac-416Vac nominal (Line-Line) 
○ Requirement is to operate at +/- 10% of nominal 
○ Minimum voltage is therefore 342VAC and maximum is 458VAC 
● Maximum current draw will be at minimum voltage at full load 
○ 38kW 3-phase at 342VAC will give a phase current of ~64A 
● Fusing should be well above this number if using fast-acting J-Type fuses 
○ Initial selection will be an 80A fuse 
○ If inadvertent tripping is detected, then a backup of 100A should be the max 
● Fuse Blocks and Disconnects should be rated to 100A with a SCCR of 100kA

##### 18 Redmond: Hot Aisle Containment (HAC) Interface

This HAC interface specification, named Redmond, is being provided to the OCP community so that they can support the deployment of high density liquid cooled racks. The main features of this interface are as follows:

● Designed to support shoulder-to-shoulder deployment of IT racks which are 28” wide, up to 50” deep, and up to 90.55 inch tall. 
● Allows the IT racks of different depths to be deployed in a front justified manner. The differences in depth are absorbed by the containment plenums. 
● Allows the IT racks of different heights to be deployed in a shoulder-to-shoulder manner. The differences in depth are absorbed by the containment plenums. 
● Accommodates rear door heat exchangers (RDHx) behind each IT rack. The RDHx is assumed to be anchored into the floor, with no physical support from the IT rack itself. 
● Allows placement of 8x 600 Amp bus ducts in the hot aisle, 4 per side. This helps deliver power to high density IT racks in a “4 makes 3” configuration.

23.1.3 Pump Reliability Testing

Long term-tests 
Long-term tests can be run on a suitable pump test-stand, ideally at the pump supplier’s 
factory. Ideally this should be a closed loop system, where pumps are connected to CDU 
hose, where precise control of the pump speed, pump pressure, flow rate and working 
fluid temperature. The test bed should be automated and continuously collect data over 
the full span of each test. 
Long term tests are designed to span the range of expected use conditions: 
- Temperature of the incoming fluid (secondary loop coolant), 25 °C and 55 °C 
- Pump Working fluid, Water-based and PG25 
- VFD (Variable Frequency Drive) power 
- CDU Hose Material 
Why is this testing important? 
● “At 25°C, PG25 is approximately 3x more viscous than deionized water. Both fluids exhibit decreasing viscosity with increasing temperature”. 
● Internal wear within the pump and pump energy consumption will depend on the fluid type and fluid temperature. 
● Degradation of the EPDM hose can occur with PG-25 and water-based coolant if an inferior hose supplier or hose type is chosen. The risk is higher at elevated temperatures. 
● Suppliers are strongly encouraged to use peroxide-cured EPDM, as it generally has better chemical resistance than sulfur-cured EPDM and is less subject to the “press-set” of the EPDM. “"Press set degradation refers to the deterioration of the material's ability to recover its original shape after being held under compression for an extended period, particularly at elevated temperatures”. 
● Example of Peroxide Cured hose designed for Data Center Applications https://www.gates.com/us/en/innovations-and-solutions/innovations/data-master hose-line.html Pump testing will be performed using two types of coolants: 
● Veolia SV5520 (deionized water + additives) or reverse osmosis water 
● DowFrost LC25 (PG25)

23.2.2 Wall-Manifold (WMF) overview

The Wall-Manifold (WMF) includes:

● Two manifolds: One supplies coolant to the cooling racks, and the other returns warm coolant from the IT racks to the CDU. 
● Multiple piping segments: These are about 3 meters long, shipped in crates, and assembled on-site to create wall manifolds up to 30 meters or more in total length. 
● A drip tray: This catches and redirects any small leaks from the wall manifolds. 
● Mounting blocks: In some cases, these connect the wall manifold to the drip tray. 
● Piping Inner diameter: of 4 inches (or larger) Conditions the Manifold Experiences: 
● Maximum Temperature of coolant in wall manifold 55 °C 
● Minimum Temperature of coolant in wall manifold 15 °C 
● Maximum working pressure for wall manifold: 130 psi 

● Maximum differential pressure (between manifold supply and return): 80-90 psi 
● A leak detection system monitors the front of server racks, the wall manifold, the Wall-Manifold rack, and each individual Wall-Manifold.

Detailed Test Procedures:1. Pressure Hold Test

● Equipment: 1 Wall-Manifold 
● Procedure: 
● Close the WMF valves and fill the system with deionized (reverse osmosis) water, removing all entrapped air. 
● Pressurize the system to 150 psi and allow the pressure to stabilize. 
● After 24 hours, record the pressure. 
● Success Criteria: 
● No leaks observed. 
● Pressure drop is no more than 10% from the initial reading. 
● No equipment damage. 
● No deformation at joints (e.g., gasket extrusion, loose tri-clamp fasteners). 
● Record any movement during testing. 2. Pressure Cycle Test 
● Equipment: Re-use the Wall-Manifold from the Pressure Hold Test. 
● Procedure: 
● Fill with RO water, remove entrapped air and Cycle the pressure from 0 to 150 psi. 
● Each cycle should take 1 to 2 minutes. 
● Perform a total of 1,000 cycles. 
● Success Criteria: 
● No leaks observed. 
● No equipment damage. 
● No deformation at joints. 
● Record any movement during testing. 3. Pressure Burst Test 
● Equipment: 1 Wall-Manifold. Re-use the Wall-Manifold from the Pressure 

Cycling Test.

● Procedure: 
● Fill with RO water, and remove all entrapped air. Increase the pressure in 50 psi increments until the Wall-Manifold fails or reaches 390 psi. 
● Success Criteria: 
● No breakage or leaking below 250 psi.

Note: Water hammer and pressure cycle tests are both used to assess the reliability and 
durability of components, but they differ in how pressure is applied and the type of stress 
they induce. 
Water Hammer Test: 
● Applies a rapid pressure surge or shock to the component. 
● Involves a sudden increase in pressure within a very short time (e.g., 0.025 seconds) followed by a return to atmospheric pressure. 
● Cycles are typically faster, for example, 2 seconds per cycle. 

● The test is intended to simulate the effects of hydraulic shock, which can occur in systems due to sudden valve closures or pump starts. 
● Additional details: 
● Water hammer testing shall be performed on “100% brand new” components that have NOT been exposed to other reliability tests. 
● Water hammer tests are faster and more severe than pressure cycling tests. Water hammer testing is generally not feasible when assembled with large volumes. Therefore, water hammer testing only at the component level is recommended. House assemblies should not be water hammer tested, but rather pressure cycle tested. 
● Special attention should be paid to the number of teeth, and tooth length on hose barbs, the clamp dimensions and the clamping forces on hoses. It is NOT acceptable for the hose to slip on the barb during pressure testing. Pressure Cycle Test: 
● Applies a gradual increase and decrease in pressure between two set points. 
● Cycles are usually slower compared to water hammer tests, for example, 1 minute per cycle. 
● The test is designed to evaluate the component's ability to withstand repeated pressure fluctuations over time.

23.4 Flammability Resistance

All materials used in Google data center wall manifolds must meet specific flammability standards to ensure safety.

Material-Specific Requirements:

● Plastic Materials: Must pass UL 94-HB flammability test (Horizontal Burning). 
● Plastic Materials: Must have a minimum V0 flammability rating. 
● Hoses: Require both V0 and VW-1 flammability ratings. 
● Cables and Wiring: Must have a minimum VW-1 flammability rating. 
● Water Sense Cable: Requires a minimum CL2P flammability rating. 

23.6 Fracture Toughness and Material Stability

On rare occasions, wall manifold materials may be accidentally dropped in the data 
center during installation. For copper and stainless steel piping, any damage is usually 
visible, and such segments are not installed. 
If non-metal materials are proposed and used for wall manifolds, the supplier must 
ensure that the Flammability Resistance specification is met and that the material does 
not creep, fatigue, or crack under expected use conditions. 

Any chosen piping material must not crack, chip, or fracture if it experiences an accidental fall in the data center. To meet this requirement, piping materials with high fracture toughness, high strength, and reasonable ductility should be selected. This is important because if a piping segment drops on the floor and develops a small, undetected, or invisible crack or tear, and the part is subsequently installed, it may fail later during use.

Most metal pipes would also suffer some damage, such as plastic deformation, from such a fall. However, a crack in metal is less likely.
