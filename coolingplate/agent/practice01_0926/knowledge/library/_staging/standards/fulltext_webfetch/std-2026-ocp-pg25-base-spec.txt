### 3 Scope

3.1 IN SCOPE 
This specification is intended for PG25 suppliers and defines the technical and quality 
requirements placed upon manufacturers of propylene glycol-based fluids (e.g. PG25) 
intended for use in single-phase cold plate cooling systems. 

Page 6

3.2 OUT OF SCOPE 
This specification does not define or mandate a common formulation across suppliers 
and is not intended to require different PG25 suppliers to produce identical products, 
nor does the specification seek to standardize proprietary inhibitor packages or additive 
chemistries. Each fluid supplier may utilize proprietary and confidential additives, 
selected based on their own formulation strategies, intellectual property and 
performance objectives. Disclosure of detailed additive composition beyond what is 
required for safety, regulatory compliance and specification conformance is not 
required. 
Compliance with this Base Specification does not imply that PG25 fluids from different 
suppliers are interchangeable or functionally equivalent. 
This specification also does not define full system‑level design requirements, nor does 
the specification provide exhaustive compatibility requirements for all possible materials 
(e.g., plastics, elastomers, seals, or adhesives) used in liquid cooling systems. 

### 4 Base Specification Overview

4.1 DESCRIPTION 
This specification establishes a foundational framework for fluid suppliers providing 
propylene glycol-based inhibited heat transfer fluids (PG25), designed for use in 
single-phase, direct-to-chip (D2C) liquid cooling systems. By defining a common 
baseline, the specification enables global consistency in production requirements and 
deployment while remaining modular and extensible within the OCP specification 
framework. This allows future workgroups to build upon the specification with 
supplemental documents addressing specific use cases, performance metrics, or 
regional considerations. 
Developed through open collaboration with fluid suppliers and industry experts, both 
within and outside the OCP community, this specification supports the broader mission 
of OCP by promoting innovation and openness in data center infrastructure. Future 
revisions will be guided by community input and evolving industry needs. 
Within the OCP ecosystem, this specification serves as a key enabler for efficient, 
scalable and sustainable liquid cooling solutions. To be compliant with this specification, 
fluid suppliers must meet the minimal criteria across the following key areas for finished 
products: 

Page 7

● Composition – Adherence to specified propylene glycol concentration and purity as well as avoidance of unapproved inhibitors 
● Product Consistency – Consistent product composition, globally, with minimal contaminants. 
● Corrosion Protection – Protection of systems metals, as defined by testing in this baseline specification 
● Physical Properties – Stability across expected operating temperatures 
● Documentation – Transparent, verifiable data on formulation, testing, and handling. 
● Manufacturing Practices – Robust quality control ensuring repeatability, traceability and compliance with industry best practices.

This specification outlines the essential requirements for PG25 suppliers to ensure 
fluids meet the minimum performance for deployment in high-performance computing

● Efficient thermal management – ensure reliable and consistent heat removal from high-density electronic components in D2C cooling systems 
● Long-term system reliability – minimize corrosion, scaling and degradation to extend the operational life of cooling systems and reduce maintenance frequency 
● Compatibility for use with common materials – ensure chemical compatibility with commonly used metals Note: It is important to consider compatibility with plastics and elastomers commonly used in liquid cooling infrastructure, however, this is not addressed in this document due to the variety of materials used in various systems. ### 5 Product Composition Requirements

PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must meet the general composition criteria outlined in this section.

5.1 PROPYLENE GLYCOL PURITY 
PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must be formulated using 
high-purity propylene glycol, such as PG USP/EP grade, with a minimum purity of 
99.5%. This ensures optimal thermal performance and system reliability. Suppliers are 
required to provide clear documentation, such as Technical Data Sheets (TDS) or 
Product Bulletins, that explicitly states: 
● Source of propylene glycol (e.g., virgin or recycled) 
● Verified purity level This transparency supports informed decision-making by end users and integrators and helps ensure consistent performance across diverse deployments. This transparency also aligns with OCP’s commitment to openness and quality assurance in infrastructure design. 
5.2 CHEMICAL COMPOSITION 
PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must contain 25–28% propylene 
glycol by weight (approximately 24.5 - 27.5% by volume), along with corrosion inhibitors 
that are compatible with common system metallurgy, including, but not limited to, 
copper, brass and stainless steel and must not degrade or damage typical plastics and 
elastomers used in Technology Cooling Systems (TCS). 
The fluid must also include a dye additive to support leak detection and fluid 
identification during operation and maintenance. 
To ensure long-term system reliability and material compatibility: 
● Silicate-based additives are prohibited, due to their potential for causing residue buildup and fouling of component surfaces. 
● Siloxane-based antifoam agents may be acceptable at concentrations below 10 ppm (as silicon) 
● Amines are not recommended, due to their potential reaction with nitrite to form nitrosamine. If nitrites are not present, the use of amines may be acceptable. 

Page 9

5.3 READY-TO-USE REQUIREMENTS 
PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must be supplied as 
ready-to-use (pre-diluted) solutions which are blended to contain 25–28% propylene 
glycol by weight (approximately 24.5 to 27.5% by volume, depending on specific 
formulation). This eliminates the need for any additional dilution, mixing or preparation 
by the end user, ensuring consistency, ease of deployment and reducing the risk of 
improper handling. 
Providing fluids in a ready-to-use format supports streamlined integration and aligns 
with OCP’s goals of efficiency, reliability and user-friendly implementation across 
diverse operational environments. 
5.4 WATER QUALITY REQUIREMENTS 
During the manufacturing process, high-purity water shall be used for blending PG25 
fluids. Manufacturers must maintain an internal specification for water quality (e.g. 
quarterly or bi-annually) and have a strategy in place to ensure water quality remains 
consistent.

maintained within these limits for routine production. Acceptable sources typically

PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must exhibit a stable pH between 
7.5 and 10.5, as measured in its ready-to-use state. The exact pH value within this 
range will depend on the specific additive package used, including corrosion inhibitors 
and stabilizers.

5.7 PRODUCT CONSISTENCY 
PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must demonstrate consistent 
formulation and quality across all regions and production batches. For any given 
product name, the fluid must maintain uniformity in: 
● Chemical composition and additive levels 
● Color and appearance 
● Performance characteristics This consistency is critical to ensure predictable behavior, system compatibility and ease of integration across global deployments. This consistency also supports OEMs and operators in managing large-scale infrastructure with confidence, reducing variability and simplifying qualification processes. Suppliers must implement robust quality control measures and maintain traceable records to verify that products meet the same standards regardless of manufacturing location. 

5.8 FINAL PRODUCT FILTRATION PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must undergo final filtration by the manufacturer prior to being transferred into containers such as totes, drums, ISO tanks, or transport vehicles. This step is crucial to maintaining fluid cleanliness standards suitable for high-performance data center cooling systems.

Page 12

Filtration must be performed using filters rated at 50 microns or finer (with 25 micron or less preferred), which are specifically approved for use with propylene glycol to avoid chemical incompatibility or degradation. To ensure consistent product quality, filters must be routinely inspected. Disposable filters must be replaced at regular intervals in accordance with documented maintenance protocols. Reusable filters (e.g., stainless steel mesh) must be routinely inspected and cleaned to maintain filtration effectiveness. This filtration requirement is justified by the need to minimize the presence of particulate matter in the final product. Low particle levels are critical to preventing fouling, clogging and wear in sensitive cooling system components, thereby ensuring long-term system reliability and performance.

### 6 Performance Requirements

6.1 OVERVIEW 
To support the use of PG25 fluids in liquid cooling systems for data center 
environments, this section outlines a baseline set of performance criteria to demonstrate 
compliance with this specification. These requirements are intended to provide 
representative performance data, such as corrosion protection and thermal stability, that 
demonstrate fundamental suitability for data center liquid cooling applications. These 
requirements are not intended to be exhaustive, nor do these requirements replace 
additional testing, qualification or validation that may be required based on specific 
system designs, operating conditions, materials of construction or customer 
requirements. Together, these minimum criteria provide a consistent reference point for 
assessing fluid performance while recognizing that broader evaluations are often 
necessary to ensure long‑term reliability and system integrity. 

6.2 CORROSION PROTECTION PG25 fluids which are compliant with this specification must be formulated with effective corrosion inhibitors to protect the internal metallurgy of liquid cooled systems, particularly components made from copper, brass and stainless steel. These additives are essential to ensure long-term system reliability and minimize degradation under use. To validate corrosion protection performance, fluids must be tested according to a modified ASTM D8040 test, utilizing copper, brass and stainless steel coupons as these

are common wetted materials. Testing must be conducted using the final product (e.g. 25% inhibited propylene glycol without further dilution) with the inclusion of corrosive salts as specified in the method. The test should be performed in triplicate at 88°C for a duration of two weeks, with continuous air bubbling through the fluid. 6.3 THERMAL STABILITY To assess thermal and chemical stability under representative stress conditions, PG25 fluids should demonstrate resistance to degradation when exposed to elevated temperatures and common sources of hardness contamination. This evaluation is performed using a modified ASTM D7437 method, which examines the effect of calcium and magnesium ions on fluid stability under thermal stress. These ions may be introduced through elastomers (e.g., hoses), dilution water or raw materials. This test is intended as a screening‑level indicator of fluid stability under thermal stress and does not, by itself, ensure long‑term operational reliability across all data center liquid cooling applications. Results from this test should be considered alongside additional performance data and system‑specific qualification requirements. The procedure involves blending 98.5 mL of PG25 fluid with 1.5 mL of hard water stock solution, as indicated in Table 4, and aging the mixture at 90 °C for two weeks in a conical glass centrifuge tube, in duplicate. At the conclusion of the test, the volume of precipitate formed is measured. Fluids producing less than 0.25 mL of precipitate are considered to demonstrate good resistance to scaling or additive breakdown

### 7 Transportation and Storage Requirements

7.1 OVERVIEW Proper handling, storage and transportation of PG25 fluids are essential to maintaining product integrity and preventing contamination. The following subsections outline the minimum requirements for preserving fluid quality throughout the supply chain.

7.2 TRANSPORTATION All PG25 fluids must be transported in a manner that safeguards the chemical stability and cleanliness. This includes implementation of strict cleaning protocols for all tanks, trucks and containers prior to loading. Residual materials from previous loads must be completely removed to prevent cross-contamination. Transport equipment must be constructed from materials compatible with the formulated product.

7.3 TOTE STORAGE 
Totes used for storage and transport must be made from materials known to be 
compatible with formulated propylene glycol and water blends, such as high-density 
polyethylene (HDPE). Totes should be stored under controlled environmental 
conditions, including: 

Page 16

● Protection from direct sunlight to avoid UV-induced breakdown of both the container and the fluid 
● Storage in temperature-stable environments to avoid thermal stress 
● Clean, dry storage areas to minimize contamination risks 

If totes are to be reused by the manufacturer, the totes must undergo a thorough cleaning protocol to eliminate all residual contaminants and ensure fluid integrity across all batches.

7.4 DRUM STORAGE Drums used for storing PG25 fluids must be constructed from materials compatible with formulated propylene glycol and water blends such as HDPE. If carbon steel drums are used, the drums must be internally lined with a suitable corrosion-resistant material to protect against degradation, particularly in the vapor space. Drums should be stored under controlled environmental conditions to preserve fluid quality. This includes:

● Protection from direct sunlight to avoid UV-induced breakdown of both the container and the fluid 
● Storage in temperature-stable environments to avoid thermal stress 
● Clean, dry storage areas to minimize contamination risks 

7.5 TANK STORAGE All tanks used for bulk storage or blending must be constructed from stainless steel, suitably lined carbon steel, HDPE or other plastics which offer the necessary chemical resistance and durability for long-term storage. Special attention must be given to the vapor space, where exposure to oxygen can promote corrosion or additive breakdown. Proper tank selection and maintenance are critical to preserving fluid quality, minimizing contamination and ensuring consistent performance across deployments.

### 8 Documentation Requirements

8.1 PRODUCT LABELING All packaged PG25 fluids must be clearly and accurately labeled to support traceability, quality assurance and regulatory compliance throughout the supply chain. This includes products stored in ISO containers, totes, drums, pails and bottles.

Page 17

Each label must include the following information:

● Product name 
● Batch or lot number 
● Date of production or expiration date Labels must be durable and resistant to premature fading or peeling. This ensures that critical product information remains legible and accessible throughout transport, storage and use. Proper labeling is essential for enabling effective inventory management and supporting root-cause analysis in the event of performance issues or recalls. 
8.2 CERTIFICATE OF ANALYSIS 
A Certificate of Analysis (COA) must accompany every shipment of PG25 fluid or be 
made readily accessible to the customer upon delivery. The COA serves as a critical 
quality assurance document, verifying that the product meets the required specifications 
for performance and consistency. 
At a minimum, the COA must include: 
● Propylene glycol concentration (expressed as % by weight or volume) 
● Alternatively, the refractive index may be provided, along with the temperature at which the measurement was taken 
● pH value of the final product, including temperature of measurement Additional data may be included to support transparency and traceability, such as batch number, production date, and results from impurity or performance testing. The COA ensures that end users can confidently validate product quality and compatibility with their systems, in alignment with OCP standards. 

8.3 TECHNICAL DATA SHEET Manufacturers must provide a Technical Data Sheet (TDS) or equivalent product literature, that clearly outlines the typical physical and thermal properties of the formulated PG25 fluid. These values must be measured directly on the final product and not sourced solely from publicly available data or base chemical references.

Page 18

At a minimum, the following properties must be disclosed, with values reported across the expected operating temperature range, including the lowest anticipated temperature the fluid may encounter (e.g., during transportation or storage):

● Viscosity 
● Heat Capacity 
● Density 
● Thermal Conductivity If publicly available data is used to supplement or support reported values, the source must be clearly cited to maintain transparency and traceability. This requirement ensures that end users have access to accurate, application-specific data for system design, performance modeling, and operational planning 

8.4 SAFETY DATA SHEET It is the responsibility of the fluid manufacturer to provide up-to-date, region-specific Safety Data Sheets (SDS) that are fully compliant with all applicable chemical safety regulations. These documents must be made readily available to customers and accompany shipments as required. SDS documentation must adhere to the standards set forth by relevant regulatory bodies, including but not limited to:

● The Globally Harmonized System (GHS) for Classification and Labeling of Chemicals 
● The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) REACH Regulation 
● The United States OSHA Hazard Communication Standard (HCS) All known hazards associated with the fluid and additive components must be accurately reported, including: 
● Toxicity 
● Environmental persistence 
● Hazard classification Manufacturers must incorporate the most current hazard and classification data available from their suppliers and recognized regulatory databases (e.g., ECHA CHEM). SDS documents must be updated in accordance with regulatory timelines, for example, within 90 days of receiving new information in the United States. Maintaining accurate and compliant SDS documentation is essential for ensuring safe handling and regulatory transparency. 

Page 19

8.5 TRACEABILITY 
To ensure traceability and support root-cause analysis in the event of a quality issue, 
suppliers must implement a comprehensive batch tracking system for all PG25 fluids. 
Each production batch must be traceable to a retained physical sample, which may 
include material from the tank, truck, drum, ISO container or other packaging format. 
These samples must be retained for a minimum of six months following production. 
Batch records, including physical property data, blend sheets manufacturing location, 
and any relevant quality control documentation, must be retained for a minimum of five 
years. 
This level of traceability ensures that any performance concerns or non-conformities 
can be investigated thoroughly and efficiently. 
8.6 ANALYTICAL RECORD RETENTION 
Suppliers are required to maintain detailed records of all analytical testing conducted on 
PG25 fluid batches produced for a minimum of three years. These records must include 
results from quality control testing and be maintained in a secure, well-organized 
manner. Each record should be clearly linked to the corresponding batch number and 
production date to ensure traceability. 
Maintaining thorough and accessible analytical documentation is essential for enabling 
root-cause investigations, supporting continuous quality assurance and ensuring 
consistency across production batches. 

### 9 Product Maintenance and Support

9.1 REMEDIATION CHEMICALS 
Fluid suppliers must be capable of providing effective solutions to restore both glycol 
concentration and inhibitor levels in the event of overdilution or depletion of inhibitors. 
This can be achieved through the following options: 
● Supplemental Chemicals - The original fluid manufacturer must offer compatible additive packages to re-inhibit the fluid when analytical parameters fall outside the manufacturer’s specified limits. 
● Concentrated Solution - A higher-concentration fluid (e.g., PG 50, PG 55, or greater) must be available from the original manufacturer to restore glycol 

Page 20

concentration if the concentration drops below 25%. This formulation must contain the same additive package as the standard PG25 fluid and be produced in accordance with the guidelines outlined in this document (e.g., water quality, documentation, analytical verification, etc.).

9.2 TECHNICAL SUPPORT 
The fluid manufacturer must offer comprehensive technical guidance on the operational

### 10 Compliance

Below is a summary of the requirements necessary to be compliant with this specification:

5 Product Composition Requirements 
5.1 Propylene 
Glycol Purity 

PG25 fluids must be formulated using high-purity propylene glycol, such as PG USP/EP grade, with a minimum purity of 99.5%. Source of propylene glycol and purity must be disclosed in documentation.

Page 22

##### 5.2 Chemical Composition

PG25 fluids must contain 25–28% propylene glycol by weight, along with corrosion inhibitors that are compatible with common system metallurgy. The fluid must also include a dye additive to support leak detection and fluid identification during operation and maintenance. Silicate-based additives are prohibited

##### 5.3 Ready-to-Use Fluid Requirements

PG25 fluids must be supplied as ready-to-use solutions which are blended to contain 25–28% propylene glycol by weight (approximately 24.5–27.5% by volume, depending on specific formulation).

##### 5.4 Water Quality Requirements

High-purity water, meeting the requirements in Table 1, must 
be used for blending PG25 fluids. Manufacturers are required 
to maintain an internal specification for water quality and 
conduct routine testing 
5.5 Fluid pH PG25 fluids must exhibit a stable pH between 7.5 and 10.5, 
as measured in its final, ready-to-use state. 
5.6 Impurities PG25 fluids must contain low levels of impurities, as 
indicated in Table 2. 

##### 5.7 Product Consistency

PG25 fluids must demonstrate consistent formulation and quality across all regions and production batches

##### 5.8 Final Product Filtration

PG25 fluids must undergo final filtration by the manufacturer prior to being transferred into containers such as totes, drums, ISO tanks or transport vehicles. Filtration must be performed using filters rated at 50 microns or finer. Note: If filtration >25 micron is used, particle size and distribution data may be required.

6 Performance Requirements 
6.2 Corrosion 
Protection 

PG25 fluids must meet or exceed the corrosion rate limits outlined in Table 3, as determined by a modified ASTM D8040 test method.

Page 23

##### 6.3 Thermal Stability

PG25 fluids must produce less than 0.25 mL of precipitate when tested according to a modified ASTM D7437 method.

7 Transportation and Storage Requirements 
7.2 Transportation PG25 fluids must be transported in a manner that 
safeguards the chemical stability and cleanliness. 
7.3 Tote Storage Totes used for storage and transport of PG25 fluids must be 
made from materials known to be compatible with 
formulated propylene glycol and water blends, such as 
high-density polyethylene (HDPE). Totes should be stored 
under controlled environmental conditions. 
7.4 Drum Storage Drums used for storing PG25 fluids must be constructed 
from materials compatible with formulated propylene glycol 
and water blends such as HDPE. If carbon steel drums are 
used, they must be internally lined with a suitable 
corrosion-resistant material to protect against degradation, 
particularly in the vapor space. Drums should be stored 
under controlled environmental conditions to preserve fluid 
quality. 
7.5 Tank Storage All tanks used for bulk storage or blending of PG25 fluids 
must be constructed from stainless steel, suitably lined 
carbon steel or HDPE, which offer the necessary chemical 
resistance and durability for long-term storage. 
8 Documentation Requirements 
8.1 Product 
Labeling 

All packaged PG25 fluids must be clearly and accurately labeled to support traceability, quality assurance and regulatory compliance throughout the supply chain.

Page 24

##### 8.2 Certificate of Analysis

A Certificate of Analysis (COA) must accompany every shipment of PG25 fluid or be made readily accessible to the customer upon delivery. This document must included propylene glycol concentration (or refractive index) and pH, at a minimum.

##### 8.3 Technical Data Sheet

Manufacturers must provide a Technical Data Sheet (TDS) or equivalent product literature, that clearly outlines the typical physical and thermal properties of the formulated PG25 fluid.

##### 8.4 Safety Data Sheet

It is the responsibility of the fluid manufacturer to provide up-to-date, region-specific Safety Data Sheets (SDS) that are fully compliant with all applicable chemical safety regulations. These documents must be made readily available to customers and accompany shipments as required. SDS documentation must adhere to the standards set forth by relevant regulatory bodies. 8.5 Tracking To ensure traceability and support root-cause analysis in the event of a quality issue, suppliers must implement a comprehensive batch tracking system for all PG25 fluids. Retains from every batch produced must be maintained for a minimum of six months, with records being retained for a minimum of five years.

##### 8.6 Analytical Record Retention

Suppliers are required to maintain detailed records of all analytical testing conducted on PG25 fluid batches produced for a minimum of three years.

9.1 Remediation 
Chemicals 

Fluid vendors must be capable of providing effective and compatible solutions to restore both glycol concentration and inhibitor levels in the event of overdilution or depletion of inhibitors.
